- **Validity:** 2026-03-09 and until further notice

## Course content

The course covers basic mathematical and statistical methods that are widely used
in modern bioinformatics.

- **Linear algebra:** matrices, vectors and eigenvalues, applied to observable and
  hidden Markov chains.
- **Changing bases**, with examples from image and signal analysis.
- **Coordinate transformations and dimensional reduction** (PCA and OLS) for
  high-dimensional data.
- **Bayesian statistics**, including fitting complex models with tools such as
  RSTAN.
- **Statistical models common in bioinformatics**, in particular the Poisson and
  negative binomial distributions.
- **Multilinear and nonlinear latent space models**, with applications in
  chemometrics and single-cell analysis.

## Learning outcomes

**Knowledge and understanding.** Students should be able to:

- explain where common statistical distributions come from
- contrast Bayesian and frequentist statistics
- describe the solution spaces of over- and underdetermined linear systems
- explain memoryless processes
- distinguish linear from nonlinear systems
- define a latent space

**Skills and abilities.** Students should be able to:

- solve systems of linear equations
- model biological problems with Markov chains
- analyse large-scale data in latent spaces, with linear and nonlinear methods
- build hierarchical models with alternative distributions, in particular Poisson
  and negative binomial
- apply chemometrics and single-cell methods to large datasets

**Judgement and approach.** Students should be able to:

- assess how valid the course's mathematical formulations are for describing
  biological systems

## Entry requirements

90 ECTS from completed courses in biology, biomedicine, molecular biology,
molecular ecology, molecular evolution, genetics, or related subjects; 5 credits in
programming in R or Python.

## Instruction

Campus-based, with lectures (pre-recorded or on site) and teacher-led sessions for
guidance on the assignments. Taught in English (Swedish if no participant needs
English). Students need their own computer.

## Examination

- An individual written on-campus exam, graded Fail (U), Pass (G) or Pass with
  distinction (VG). The exam grade is the course grade.
- The course coordinator may award bonus points for optional activities, up to
  10 % of the exam score. Which activities count, and how, is announced at the
  start of the course.
- After two failed attempts, a student may request a new examiner from the Deputy
  Head of the Department of Molecular Biology.
- The examiner may adapt the exam format to a student's needs, within the learning
  outcomes. Requests must be made at least 10 working days before the exam.

## Transitional provisions

If the syllabus expires or is substantially revised, students keep at least three
exam opportunities under the original syllabus, for up to two years.
