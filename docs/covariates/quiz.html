Quiz: design matrices for covariates

Short questions to test your understanding of design matrices: building \(X\) from a formula, computing \(X\boldsymbol\beta\), reading coefficients, and deciding whether \(\boldsymbol\beta\) has a unique solution. Try each on paper first, then open the answer. Most are in the style of the on-campus exam (no computer).

Q1. A study of one gene uses the formula expr ~ sex + batch + bmi, where sex is F/M and the sequencing batch has levels A, B, C. How many columns does the design matrix \(X\) have? Name them.

Hint

Intercept: 1 column. Factor with \(k\) levels: \(k - 1\) columns. Number: 1 column.

Answer

\(1 + 1 + 2 + 1 = 5\) columns: (Intercept), sexM, batchB, batchC, bmi. F and A are the reference levels and get no column of their own.

Q2. For the model in Q1 the fitted coefficients are \(\boldsymbol\beta = (3,\ 1,\ 2,\ -1,\ 0.5)\), in the column order above. Write the row of \(X\) for a man with BMI 24, sequenced in batch C, and compute his predicted expression.

Hint

Walk through the columns in order: 1, then the sex dummy, then the two batch dummies, then the BMI value. Then take the dot product with \(\boldsymbol\beta\).

Answer

Row: \((1,\ 1,\ 0,\ 1,\ 24)\). Prediction: \(3 \cdot 1 + 1 \cdot 1 + 2 \cdot 0 + (-1) \cdot 1 + 0.5 \cdot 24 = 3 + 1 + 0 - 1 + 12 = 15\).

Q3. In the same model, say in one sentence each what batchC \(= -1\) and bmi \(= 0.5\) mean.

Hint

A dummy’s coefficient compares with the reference level. A number’s coefficient is a slope.

Answer

batchC \(= -1\): samples from batch C are predicted 1 unit lower than batch A (the reference), for the same sex and BMI. bmi \(= 0.5\): each extra BMI unit raises the predicted expression by 0.5, for the same sex and batch.

Q4. An experiment measures one gene at three time points after stimulation. The model expr ~ time (time a factor with reference 0 h) gives (Intercept) \(= 6\), time6h \(= 4\), time24h \(= -2\). What are the predicted means at 0 h, 6 h and 24 h? Could a model with time as a number (expr ~ hours) give the same three means?

Hint

Each time point’s mean is the intercept plus its own dummy’s coefficient. A number column means a straight line.

Answer

0 h: \(6\); 6 h: \(6 + 4 = 10\); 24 h: \(6 - 2 = 4\). No: a straight line in hours cannot go up from 0 h to 6 h and then down from 6 h to 24 h. The factor coding allows any shape; the numeric coding forces one slope.

Q5. Write the interaction column treatmenttreat:sexM for the four groups F/ctrl, F/treat, M/ctrl, M/treat. With \(\boldsymbol\beta = (2,\ 3,\ 1,\ 2)\) for ((Intercept), treatmenttreat, sexM, treatmenttreat:sexM), what is the predicted mean of treated males?

Hint

The interaction column is the product of the two dummy columns.

Answer

The column is \((0,\ 0,\ 0,\ 1)\): it is 1 only when both dummies are 1 (treated males). Treated males: row \((1,\ 1,\ 1,\ 1)\), so \(\hat y = 2 + 3 + 1 + 2 = 8\).

Q6. Why does R give a factor with two levels (F, M) only one dummy column, sexM, and not a column for each level?

Hint

Add the two columns sexF and sexM together. What do you get?

Answer

Because sexF + sexM would equal the intercept column (every sample is F or M). The columns would be linearly dependent, and infinitely many \(\boldsymbol\beta\) would give the same fit (the dummy-variable trap). Dropping the reference level’s column keeps the columns independent, so \(\boldsymbol\beta\) is unique.

Q7. A lab sequenced all 4 control samples in batch A and all 4 treated samples in batch B, and fits expr ~ batch + treatment. (a) Does \(\boldsymbol\beta\) have a unique solution? Point to the problem in \(X\). (b) What does R’s lm() report, and what does DESeq2 do? (c) What should the lab have done?

Hint

Write the columns batchB and treatmenttreat side by side.

Answer
  1. No. The columns batchB and treatmenttreat are identical (both are 0 for samples 1–4 and 1 for samples 5–8), so only their sum is determined: infinitely many \(\boldsymbol\beta\) fit equally well, and the batch effect cannot be told apart from the treatment effect.
  2. lm() reports NA for treatmenttreat (the batchB coefficient then carries batch and treatment together). DESeq2 stops with the error “the model matrix is not full rank”.
  3. Spread both conditions over both batches (e.g. 2 control + 2 treated in each). This is a design problem; it cannot be fixed in the analysis.

Q8. A pilot study has 5 patients. The analyst proposes expr ~ treatment + sex + age + bmi + batch with batch A/B/C. Is \(\boldsymbol\beta\) unique? Give a reason in one sentence.

Hint

Count the columns and compare with the number of samples.

Answer

No: \(X\) has \(1 + 1 + 1 + 1 + 1 + 2 = 7\) columns but only 5 rows, and with 5 rows at most 5 columns can be linearly independent (\(p > n\)). Use fewer covariates or more patients.

Q9. In expr ~ treatment * sex the samples are: F/ctrl, F/ctrl, F/treat, F/treat, M/ctrl, M/ctrl. Is \(\boldsymbol\beta\) unique? If not, what are two ways to fix it?

Hint

Fill in the interaction column for each sample.

Answer

No. There are no treated males, so the treatmenttreat:sexM column is all zeros, and its coefficient can take any value without changing the fit (an empty cell). Fixes: drop the interaction (expr ~ treatment + sex, which is unique here), or collect treated males so that every combination is observed.
