Course syllabus — 3MB00A

Summary of the official syllabus, which is the binding version: https://www.umu.se/en/education/syllabus/3mb00a/

Course identification

  • English title: Quantitative Biology and Integrative Biology
  • Swedish title: Kvantitativ biologi och integrativ omik
  • Course code: 3MB00A
  • Credits: 7.5 ECTS
  • Education level: Second cycle
  • Main field of study: Bioinformatics (second cycle, entry-level requirements only)
  • Grading scale: Three-grade scale (U / G / VG)
  • Responsible department: Department of Molecular Biology
  • Established: 2026-01-21 by the education board of the Faculty of Medicine
  • Validity: 2026-03-09 and until further notice

Course content

The course covers basic mathematical and statistical methods that are widely used in modern bioinformatics.

  • Linear algebra: matrices, vectors and eigenvalues, applied to observable and hidden Markov chains.
  • Changing bases, with examples from image and signal analysis.
  • Coordinate transformations and dimensional reduction (PCA and OLS) for high-dimensional data.
  • Bayesian statistics, including fitting complex models with tools such as RSTAN.
  • Statistical models common in bioinformatics, in particular the Poisson and negative binomial distributions.
  • Multilinear and nonlinear latent space models, with applications in chemometrics and single-cell analysis.

Learning outcomes

Knowledge and understanding. Students should be able to:

  • explain where common statistical distributions come from
  • contrast Bayesian and frequentist statistics
  • describe the solution spaces of over- and underdetermined linear systems
  • explain memoryless processes
  • distinguish linear from nonlinear systems
  • define a latent space

Skills and abilities. Students should be able to:

  • solve systems of linear equations
  • model biological problems with Markov chains
  • analyse large-scale data in latent spaces, with linear and nonlinear methods
  • build hierarchical models with alternative distributions, in particular Poisson and negative binomial
  • apply chemometrics and single-cell methods to large datasets

Judgement and approach. Students should be able to:

  • assess how valid the course’s mathematical formulations are for describing biological systems

Entry requirements

90 ECTS from completed courses in biology, biomedicine, molecular biology, molecular ecology, molecular evolution, genetics, or related subjects; 5 credits in programming in R or Python.

Instruction

Campus-based, with lectures (pre-recorded or on site) and teacher-led sessions for guidance on the assignments. Taught in English (Swedish if no participant needs English). Students need their own computer.

Examination

  • An individual written on-campus exam, graded Fail (U), Pass (G) or Pass with distinction (VG). The exam grade is the course grade.
  • The course coordinator may award bonus points for optional activities, up to 10 % of the exam score. Which activities count, and how, is announced at the start of the course.
  • After two failed attempts, a student may request a new examiner from the Deputy Head of the Department of Molecular Biology.
  • The examiner may adapt the exam format to a student’s needs, within the learning outcomes. Requests must be made at least 10 working days before the exam.

Transitional provisions

If the syllabus expires or is substantially revised, students keep at least three exam opportunities under the original syllabus, for up to two years.
